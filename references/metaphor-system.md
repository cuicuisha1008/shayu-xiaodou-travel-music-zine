# Music Metaphor System

## Core Test

A valid metaphor must satisfy all three:

1. **Structural evidence:** at least three source features map to functional parts of the music system.
2. **Emotional evidence:** the listening or recording action matches the photograph's mood.
3. **Material evidence:** the lower object can be built from faithful source fragments without warping people or rewriting the scene.

Do not mistake proximity for metaphor. A record placed beside a beach photo is decoration; water reflections becoming grooves, figures becoming sample points, and a rock becoming a stylus is a metaphor.

## Structural Vocabulary

Use this as a possibility space, not a lookup table.

| Visible structure | Possible music relation | Functional mapping examples |
|---|---|---|
| Two people or two dominant subjects | stereo / duet / call-and-response | left and right channels, two earcups, paired meters |
| Two circles, boats, windows, pools, or suns | cassette / reel-to-reel | reel hubs, tape counters, paired spools |
| Circular reflection, plaza, window, plate, moon | vinyl / CD | grooves, center label, spindle, spectral rim |
| Long road, river, wake, railing, cable, coastline | tape / timeline / signal path | magnetic ribbon, playhead path, waveform baseline |
| Repeated buoys, lamps, windows, pillars, steps | sequencer / rhythm grid / EQ | beat markers, step notes, fader bands, track divisions |
| Layered mountains, roofs, waves, horizons | multitrack arrangement | stacked tracks, frequency bands, score staves |
| Tall tower, tree, monument, vertical street | waveform peak / fader / level meter | signal amplitude, mixer channel, spectrum tower |
| Frame, doorway, hotel window, pool rectangle | album packaging / jewel case | cover window, booklet, track insert, blue CD |
| Rain, glitter, dust, crowds, small lights | record noise / granular sample | groove texture, sample cloud, spectral grains |
| Shadow silhouettes and backlight | field recording / sampled voices | sample markers, cue points, ghost tracks |
| Reflection or mirror pair | echo / reverb / delay | dry-wet pair, delayed copy, feedback trail |
| Train, boat, car, moving crowd | tempo / playback / mixtape | transport controls, side A/B, moving playhead |
| Architecture with repeated rooms | sampler bank / track list | pads, channels, album sequencing |

Prefer a less obvious relation when it is better supported. A photograph may become a mixing console, MiniDisc, spectrum display, lyric booklet, concert ticket, DAT tape, field recorder, turntable, or stereo panorama—not only a record, CD, cassette, or headphones.

## Copy Formula

Build the proposition from three parts:

`[visible force or place] + [precise musical action] + [time / people / memory]`

Useful tensions:

- landscape acts, people become sound;
- a place stores a season;
- distance becomes stereo separation;
- repetition becomes rhythm;
- reflection becomes recording surface;
- a path becomes playback time;
- a crowd becomes noise while one figure becomes melody;
- silence becomes the loudest track.

Avoid generic slogans such as “旅行是一首歌”, “聆听世界”, “音乐与远方”, or “每一刻都值得记录”. Avoid sentimental explanation after the conceptual turn.

## Metadata Language

Use metadata as atmosphere, not fabricated evidence. Safe patterns:

- `SIDE A / SIDE B`
- `FIELD RECORDING`
- `TRAVEL ARCHIVE`
- `STEREO / MONO`
- `TRACK 01`
- `COASTAL TAPE`, `CITY MIX`, `NIGHT PRESSING`

Use exact location or time only when the user supplies it or source metadata is deliberately inspected and appropriate to expose. Never copy camera metadata into the poster without a reason.
